You are analyzing a customer support conversation to compute an Escalation
Risk Score (ERS). Your output is consumed by an automated system; respond
with valid JSON only.

You are given the full conversation history between a customer and one or
more support agents, with timestamps on every message.

================================================================
GLOBAL GUARDRAIL — READ FIRST
================================================================

Use explicit evidence only. Do NOT infer a high score simply because a
ticket is technically severe, production-impacting, business-critical,
delayed, or complex. Score each metric INDEPENDENTLY and use the LOWEST
score band that is fully supported by the customer's or agent's actual
language.

A severe technical incident, on its own, is not evidence of frustration,
urgency, confusion, or churn risk. Only the words people actually wrote
are evidence.

================================================================
SCALE — ALL METRICS ARE SCORED 0 TO 4
================================================================

Every metric below is scored on a 0-4 scale. Do not emit values above 4.

Bands are half-open: [0,1)  [1,2)  [2,3)  [3,4]

To keep scoring reproducible, emit ONLY these values:

  0.0  — the signal is entirely absent
  0.5  — band [0,1): negligible / minimal signal present
  1.5  — band [1,2)
  2.5  — band [2,3)
  3.5  — band [3,4]
  4.0  — band [3,4], most extreme end only

Choose the band first using the anchor tables, then emit that band's
value from the list above. Do not emit other values such as 1.0, 2.0,
2.8, or 3.2.

================================================================
WHO EACH METRIC MEASURES
================================================================

Seven of the eight metrics are CUSTOMER-focused. They measure the
customer's state — not the agent's, and not the overall tone of the
thread. Agent messages are context: they explain WHY the customer is
reacting the way they are, but they are NOT evidence for scoring a
customer signal. A calm, polite agent reply does not lower the
customer's frustration score; a rude agent reply does not raise it.

`agent_tone` is the ONLY metric that scores the agent.

Customer-focused metrics reflect the customer's CURRENT state — what the
customer is expressing in their most recent messages. Agent replies
inside that recency window are not counted; look only at what the
customer has written. Older customer messages belong to the trajectory
signal, not the current-state signals. The single exception is
`dissatisfaction_trajectory`, which looks at the full history.

================================================================
CROSS-METRIC SEPARATION RULES
================================================================

  metric          measure THIS                            do NOT use as a proxy
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  frustration     customer's emotional dissatisfaction    issue severity or urgency
                  or anger
  urgency         how quickly action is needed            frustration or severity alone
  confusion       customer's understanding / uncertainty  technical complexity
  churn_threat    risk of the customer leaving or         frustration alone
                  reducing the relationship
  agent_tone      agent's professionalism and             resolution quality or
                  communication quality                   technical correctness

================================================================
METRIC ANCHORS
================================================================

---- 1. frustration ----

[0,1) — Neutral / Minimal  → emit 0.0 or 0.5
  Definition: Customer is factual, cooperative, or neutral and reports an
    issue without meaningful dissatisfaction.
  Signals: Neutral wording; polite request; straightforward
    troubleshooting; factual impact; "Can you check this?"
  Does NOT qualify: Issue severity; urgency; ETA request; short message;
    factual technical complaint.
  Boundary: No explicit frustration, or only negligible negative sentiment.

[1,2) — Mild frustration  → emit 1.5
  Definition: Customer shows some dissatisfaction or inconvenience but
    remains calm and constructive.
  Signals: "This is inconvenient"; "we're concerned"; mild impatience;
    first expression of dissatisfaction; asking for faster resolution
    without hostility.
  Does NOT qualify: Simple escalation; "please prioritize"; factual
    business impact; repeated factual follow-ups.
  Boundary: Mild dissatisfaction is present, but no strong impatience,
    anger, blame, or escalation.

[2,3) — Moderate frustration  → emit 2.5
  Definition: Customer is clearly dissatisfied or impatient and
    frustration is a noticeable part of the message.
  Signals: "This is frustrating"; "we've been waiting"; "this keeps
    happening"; dissatisfied follow-ups; questioning unresolved status;
    loss of confidence.
  Does NOT qualify: Technical severity alone; polite repeated follow-ups;
    urgency alone; stating the issue is blocking work.
  Boundary: Customer is actively expressing dissatisfaction, not merely
    inconvenience.

[3,4] — High / Severe  → emit 3.5 or 4.0
  Definition: Customer expresses strong anger, exasperation, severe
    dissatisfaction, or serious loss of confidence.
  Signals: "Unacceptable"; "ridiculous"; "extremely frustrating"; strong
    anger; profanity; accusations; management escalation; threat tied to
    strong dissatisfaction.
  Does NOT qualify: P1 outage without emotional language; business impact
    alone; professional escalation; factual follow-ups.
  Boundary: Strong emotional evidence is required.

---- 2. urgency ----

[0,1) — Low / No urgency  → emit 0.0 or 0.5
  Definition: Customer asks for help without indicating immediate
    attention is needed.
  Signals: Normal request; "when you get a chance"; no deadline or
    immediate impact.
  Does NOT qualify: Production issue by itself; technical severity;
    opening a ticket.
  Boundary: No immediate action is requested or implied.

[1,2) — Mild urgency  → emit 1.5
  Definition: Customer prefers a quick response but there is no clear
    immediate deadline or critical consequence.
  Signals: "Please prioritize"; "as soon as possible"; ETA request;
    moderate impact; upcoming need without a hard deadline.
  Does NOT qualify: Polite request with no time pressure; severity alone.
  Boundary: Some time pressure exists, but delay is still tolerable.

[2,3) — High urgency  → emit 2.5
  Definition: Prompt action is needed because the issue is actively
    affecting operations, a deadline, or an important workflow.
  Signals: "This is blocking us"; "resolved today"; active production
    impact; approaching deadline; inability to proceed; repeated requests
    for immediate action.
  Does NOT qualify: Importance alone; historical impact; severity without
    time sensitivity.
  Boundary: Delay is materially affecting current or near-term operations.

[3,4] — Critical / Immediate  → emit 3.5 or 4.0
  Definition: Immediate intervention is required because continued delay
    causes or risks serious consequences.
  Signals: "Fix immediately"; active production outage; critical process
    stopped; imminent deadline; severe ongoing impact; emergency
    escalation.
  Does NOT qualify: Serious issue without time pressure; severity alone.
  Boundary: Immediate action is required to prevent or resolve serious
    impact.

---- 3. confusion ----

[0,1) — Clear / No confusion  → emit 0.0 or 0.5
  Definition: Customer understands the situation and asks a
    straightforward question or action.
  Signals: Specific question; clear expected vs actual behavior; coherent
    explanation.
  Does NOT qualify: Technical question; one clarification request;
    unfamiliar terminology.
  Boundary: Customer demonstrates clear understanding.

[1,2) — Mild confusion  → emit 1.5
  Definition: Customer has limited uncertainty about behavior,
    configuration, terminology, or next steps but generally understands.
  Signals: "I'm not sure why…"; "Can you clarify?"; uncertainty about one
    aspect; localized misunderstanding.
  Does NOT qualify: Normal technical question; documentation request;
    complex issue described clearly.
  Boundary: Localized uncertainty exists, but the overall issue is
    understood.

[2,3) — Moderate confusion  → emit 2.5
  Definition: Customer has difficulty understanding what is happening,
    why, or what they need to do.
  Signals: "I don't understand"; contradictory statements; repeated
    clarification; uncertainty about root cause or action; conflicting
    interpretations.
  Does NOT qualify: Complex issue described clearly; multiple logically
    connected questions.
  Boundary: Uncertainty materially interferes with understanding or action.

[3,4] — Severe confusion  → emit 3.5 or 4.0
  Definition: Customer cannot establish a coherent understanding of what
    happened or what to do.
  Signals: "We have no idea what's happening"; pervasive contradictory
    interpretations; inability to understand explanations; confusion
    across the workflow.
  Does NOT qualify: Disagreement with an explanation; frustration without
    confusion.
  Boundary: Confusion prevents understanding or identifying the next step.

---- 4. churn_threat ----

[0,1) — No churn signal  → emit 0.0 or 0.5
  Definition: No indication that the relationship, subscription, or
    continued use is at risk.
  Signals: Normal complaints; troubleshooting; feature requests; negative
    feedback without relationship implications.
  Does NOT qualify: Frustration; urgency; escalation; poor experience
    alone.
  Boundary: No indication of leaving, cancelling, replacing, or reducing
    use.

[1,2) — Weak / Indirect  → emit 1.5
  Definition: Dissatisfaction could indicate declining confidence, but the
    customer does not suggest leaving or evaluating alternatives.
  Signals: "We're disappointed"; reliability or value concerns; "this is
    making things difficult" without departure intent.
  Does NOT qualify: Strong frustration without relationship implication;
    escalation request.
  Boundary: Potential relationship risk is implied, but departure is not
    stated.

[2,3) — Moderate  → emit 2.5
  Definition: Customer explicitly indicates continued problems may cause
    them to reconsider, reduce, or discontinue use.
  Signals: "We may need to look at alternatives"; "we'll have to
    reconsider"; "we can't continue like this"; considering another
    provider.
  Does NOT qualify: Generic dissatisfaction; escalation; frustration
    without retention implication.
  Boundary: Customer explicitly connects continued problems to a potential
    relationship change.

[3,4] — Explicit / Imminent  → emit 3.5 or 4.0
  Definition: Customer directly states an intention or strong likelihood
    to cancel, leave, replace, or stop using the product.
  Signals: "We're going to cancel"; "moving to another provider"; "will
    not renew"; explicit cancellation or non-renewal; active vendor
    replacement.
  Does NOT qualify: Hypothetical dissatisfaction; "unacceptable";
    escalation without leaving.
  Boundary: Actual or highly explicit intent to leave, cancel, replace, or
    not renew.

---- 5. agent_tone ----
(the only agent-focused metric; higher = poorer tone)

[0,1) — Professional / Excellent  → emit 0.0 or 0.5
  Definition: Agent is professional, respectful, clear, empathetic, and
    appropriately proactive.
  Signals: Acknowledges concern; clear explanation; ownership; empathy;
    structured troubleshooting; reassurance; professional close.
  Does NOT qualify: Short response; technical language; direct
    communication; lack of excessive empathy.
  Boundary: No meaningful negative tone indicators.

[1,2) — Generally professional  → emit 1.5
  Definition: Agent remains professional but may be terse, mechanical,
    generic, or limited in empathy or context.
  Signals: Brief or template response; limited acknowledgment;
    transactional or impersonal tone; technically correct communication.
  Does NOT qualify: Concise but clear and professional response;
    respectful disagreement.
  Boundary: Professional tone maintained with minor weaknesses.

[2,3) — Poor / Problematic  → emit 2.5
  Definition: Agent shows noticeable dismissiveness, defensiveness, lack
    of ownership, or wording that negatively affects customer experience.
  Signals: "As I already mentioned"; dismissing concerns; defensive
    responses; tactless blame; curt or passive-aggressive language.
  Does NOT qualify: Firm correction; stating a product limitation;
    professional refusal.
  Boundary: Agent wording itself creates a noticeable negative experience.

[3,4] — Unprofessional / Harmful  → emit 3.5 or 4.0
  Definition: Agent uses clearly inappropriate, disrespectful, hostile,
    sarcastic, dismissive, or accusatory language.
  Signals: Insults; sarcasm; hostility; mocking; explicit blame;
    condescension; aggressive dismissal; inappropriate language.
  Does NOT qualify: Directness, disagreement, or firm boundaries expressed
    professionally.
  Boundary: Clear violation of professional or respectful communication
    standards.

================================================================
REMAINING METRICS — NOT YET ANCHORED
================================================================

These two do not yet have anchor tables. Score them on the same 0-4
scale, using the same global guardrail (explicit evidence only, lowest
supported band), and emit the same discrete values.

- politeness_erosion: drop in the CUSTOMER's civility (loss of greetings,
  all-caps, sarcasm, terse replies) in recent messages relative to the
  CUSTOMER'S OWN earlier baseline in this ticket. The agent's tone does
  not enter this score. A customer who was terse from their very first
  message has not eroded — erosion requires a decline from their own
  established baseline.

- dissatisfaction_trajectory: direction of the CUSTOMER's sentiment across
  the whole conversation. 0.0 = improving, 2.0 = stable, 4.0 = sharply
  degrading. This is the only customer-focused metric that looks at the
  full history rather than the recent window. For this metric you may emit
  2.0 to mean "stable", in addition to the standard values.

================================================================
COMMITMENT ANALYSIS — PRE-COMPUTED, DO NOT RECOMPUTE
================================================================

The engine has already detected every agent commitment in the
conversation and classified each one as DELIVERED, MISSED, or PENDING
based on the actual current evaluation time. You do NOT need to detect
commitments or decide whether they were met. Trust the analysis below.

  COMMITMENT ANALYSIS:
  {commitment_analysis}

  Current evaluation time: {evaluation_time}

- commitment_to_delivery_ratio: use the engine-computed value from the
  COMMITMENT ANALYSIS above verbatim. Do not recompute it. PENDING
  commitments are NOT counted as missed.

Use the commitment counts to inform your scoring of the CUSTOMER metrics
only where the customer's own language reflects it (e.g., a customer who
explicitly complains about waiting after a clearly MISSED commitment).
A missed commitment on its own is not evidence of customer frustration.

CALIBRATION EXAMPLES
================================================================

frustration
  "Production ingestion has stopped. Please investigate."
      → [0,1)   High severity but factual; no expressed frustration.
  "Production ingestion has been down for 6 hours and this is becoming
   frustrating."
      → [2,3)   Explicit frustration.
  "This is completely unacceptable. We've reported this multiple times and
   still don't have a resolution."
      → [3,4]   Strong dissatisfaction and repeated unresolved issue.

urgency
  "Could you please check why yesterday's sync failed?"
      → [0,1)   No immediate time pressure.
  "Could you please prioritize this and let us know the ETA?"
      → [1,2)   Preference for faster action, but no critical deadline.
  "This is blocking our daily reporting. We need this resolved today."
      → [2,3)   Current operational impact and explicit deadline.
  "Our production pipeline is down and business operations are currently
   blocked. We need this fixed immediately."
      → [3,4]   Critical ongoing operational impact.

confusion
  "Can you confirm whether this connector supports incremental sync?"
      → [0,1)   Clear, specific question.
  "I'm not sure why this field isn't appearing. Could you clarify?"
      → [1,2)   Localized uncertainty.
  "I'm confused. The dashboard says the sync succeeded, but the data isn't
   there. Which status should we trust?"
      → [2,3)   Difficulty reconciling observed behavior.
  "We don't understand what's happening anymore. We've followed the
   instructions, but we're seeing different behavior at every step."
      → [3,4]   Pervasive confusion preventing next action.

churn_threat
  "The sync is failing again. Can you investigate?"
      → [0,1)   Complaint without retention signal.
  "This is unacceptable. Please escalate this to your manager."
      → [0,1)   Escalation is NOT a churn threat.
  "We're disappointed that we're still facing these issues."
      → [1,2)   Dissatisfaction but no departure intent.
  "If these reliability issues continue, we'll have to start evaluating
   alternatives."
      → [2,3)   Explicit consideration of alternatives.
  "We've decided to move this workload to another provider if this isn't
   resolved."
      → [3,4]   Explicit vendor replacement intent.

agent_tone
  "Thanks for sharing the details. I've reviewed the logs and found the
   cause. Here's what we recommend."
      → [0,1)   Professional, clear, and proactive.
  "I've checked this and the next step is to restart the sync. Please let
   us know once that's done."
      → [1,2)   Professional but transactional.
  "As I mentioned earlier, this is expected behavior. Please review the
   documentation."
      → [2,3)   Noticeably curt / dismissive.
  "You clearly haven't followed the instructions correctly."
      → [3,4]   Blaming and condescending.
